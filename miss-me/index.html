The novel

A private world
without simple answers

Intimate, unsettling, and threaded with mordant humour, I’m Going to Miss Me enters the private world of a narrator living at a distance from the people and expectations surrounding him.

It refuses easy explanations. Instead, it stays with the thoughts people edit out before speaking.

The manuscript

A manuscript
left behind

Days after Lee’s death, Andy received a manuscript, instructions to seek publication, and one clear directive: protect the author’s identity.

He became the unexpected custodian of a story whose author no longer wished to be known.
No portraitNo biographyOnly a chosen name

About Lee

The name
that remains

Lee Knoll is the pseudonym of the novel’s late author. Their identity remains private in accordance with their final wishes.

No public biography, portrait, or personal history will be used to replace the anonymity they deliberately chose.

Andy’s afterword

Why I brought this
book into the world

To this day, I remain mystified about why I was chosen to bring a story into the world written by a person I hardly knew.

I had been drafted, and this was my assignment.

After repeated readings, I am resigned to not knowing. But I wonder if Lee’s greatest fear was being misunderstood.
